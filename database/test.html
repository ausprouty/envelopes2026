One of the reasons I encourage people to discover the Bible in groups is simple:

We see more clearly together.

When one person studies Scripture alone—or when everyone simply accepts what one strong teacher says—it can be surprisingly easy for an idea to drift away from what the passage actually says.

But in a group, people notice different things. Someone may make an observation, and another person can ask, “Where do you see that in the passage?” Together they keep coming back to God’s Word.

I was reminded of this recently while snorkeling in Hawaii.

Chris spotted something on the ocean floor that neither of us recognized. It looked like a collection of long white lines radiating out from one point.

I tried describing it to ChatGPT. It suggested one creature. Chris looked at the picture and said, “No, that’s not it.”

So we looked more carefully.

The tentacles were lying flat on the ocean floor. They were not feathery. They looked more like worms.

With those additional observations, we tried again and again until we eventually identified it: a spaghetti worm.

Neither Chris nor I had the whole answer at first. But by observing, questioning and comparing what we were seeing, we eventually arrived at the right conclusion.

That is one of the things I enjoy about studying Scripture with a group.

Each time we meet we ask:

What does this passage tell us about God?
What does it tell us about people?
If this is true, how should it change the way we live?

One person notices something. Another sees something they missed. Someone suggests an interpretation, and another person asks, “Where do you see that in the passage?”

The authority stays with Scripture rather than with the strongest personality in the room.

This is one reason that in the MyFriends Lifestyle we encourage people to discover the Bible together.

But you do not have to wait for a group.

If you know just one person who wants to learn more about God, start with them. You could meet regularly and discuss Life Issues.

Then, when the time seems right, ask:

“Who else do you know who might be interested in discovering for themselves what the Bible says about spiritual community?”

This helps form a group from people who already know each other. They already have relationships outside the Bible study, so they are more likely to spend time together between meetings as well.

Once the group has formed, move into Discovering Spiritual Community.

One simple way to begin is to ask the person you are already meeting with:

“Who else do you know who might be interested in discovering for themselves what the Bible says about spiritual community?”

That is how we started our first group.

You do not need to have a crowd before you begin. Start with the person God has already given you, and then watch for the people they may bring with them.
